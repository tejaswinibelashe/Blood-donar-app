Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to the most common questions about blood donation and the BloodLink platform.

Most people can give blood if they are in good health, between 18 and 65 years old, and weigh at least 50 kg.

Men can donate whole blood every 12 weeks, and women can donate every 16 weeks.

Yes, absolutely. New, sterile disposable equipment is used for each donor, so there is no risk of contracting a blood-borne infection.

You will feel a slight pinch when the needle is inserted, but it only lasts a second. Most donors do not find it painful.

About 450ml (one pint) of blood is drawn, which is roughly 10% of the body's total volume. The body replenishes this fluid within 24 hours.

The actual blood draw takes about 8-10 minutes, but the entire process including registration and screening takes about 45-60 minutes.

Eat a healthy, low-fat meal and drink plenty of fluids (water or juice) before you donate. Avoid alcohol and fatty foods.

It is recommended to avoid strenuous physical activity or heavy lifting for about 24 hours after donating.
